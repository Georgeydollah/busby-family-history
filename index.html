The Busby Family History

A continuous legacy stretching from the 13th century to the present day. The earliest recorded Oxfordshire Busby was Alexander Busby of Milcombe, who purchased a 'hide' from Eynsham Abbey in 1241[cite: 21]. Below is the direct ancestral line bridging Deddington and Hook Norton.

Direct Ancestors

Generation 1: John Busby (Died c. 1558)

John Busby of Clifton was a husbandman and died circa 1558[cite: 21]. His will requested his body to be buried in Deddington churchyard[cite: 21].

Generation 2: Simon Busby (Died c. 1598)

Simon Busby died circa 1598[cite: 22]. He left ten shillings to the poor of Clifton and Deddington, and a bushel of barley to Deddington church[cite: 22].

Generation 3: William Busby (Died c. 1609)

William Busby died circa 1609 and was buried in Deddington churchyard[cite: 23]. His widow, Elizabeth, lived long enough to remarry a Mr. Gilks[cite: 24].

Generation 4: Simon Busby (Died 1650)

Simon Busby died in 1650[cite: 25]. His wife Anne was recorded in the 1665 Oxfordshire Hearth Tax as having three hearths, suggesting they lived in a house with six to eleven rooms and were of the yeoman class[cite: 25].

Generation 5: Robert Busby (Baptized 1644)

Robert Busby was baptized in Deddington church in November 1644[cite: 28]. He married Mary Reade of Enstone in 1682[cite: 28]. Mary is almost certainly the ancestor who bought the family property in Hook Norton in 1702[cite: 29].

Generation 6: Robert Busby (Baptized 1684)

Robert Busby was baptized in Deddington church[cite: 19]. He married Sarah Spelsbury in 1705[cite: 19].

Generation 7: Benjamin Busby (Baptized 1721)

Benjamin Busby was baptized in Dunstew in 1721[cite: 18]. A 1765 Settlement Certificate provided the vital link connecting the Hook Norton family back to Deddington[cite: 18].

Generation 8: Benjamin Busby (Baptized 1759)

Benjamin Busby was baptized in Hook Norton church in 1759[cite: 16]. He continued the family presence in the house now known as Laburnum Cottage on Queen Street[cite: 16].

Generation 9: Benjamin Busby (Baptized 1796)

Benjamin Busby was baptized in Hook Norton church on May 5th 1796[cite: 12]. He married Anna Prophett, the daughter of a well-established Quaker family, in 1822[cite: 12].

Generation 10: Henry Busby (Born 1825)

Henry Busby was born in 1825 and became a master slater and plasterer[cite: 9]. He married Mary Ann Moulder in 1849[cite: 9].

Generation 11: Henry Busby (Born 1855)

Henry Busby was born in Hook Norton in 1855 and followed his father into the building trade[cite: 4]. He founded the Hook Norton Temperance Band, which featured so many family members it became known as the 'Busby band'[cite: 4].

Dr. John Walter Busby

A doctor of medicine who solidified his branch of the family's legacy by successfully petitioning the College of Arms and being granted an official Coat of Arms in 1976.

Notable Connections

The following individuals share historical ties to the Busby name or are uniquely connected to our family history.

Sir John Busby

A lawyer who worked for the Royalists and the Verney family in Buckinghamshire. Because of his service, he was granted a knighthood and a family crest. While not in the direct paternal lineage, his crest was adopted by many later Busbys who proudly, if unofficially, carved it onto their tombstones in Oxfordshire.

Dr. Richard Busby (1606–1695)

An English Anglican priest who served as the famously strict Headmaster of Westminster School for over 55 years.

Sir Christopher Wren (1632–1723)

One of the most highly acclaimed architects in history, responsible for rebuilding 52 churches in London after the Great Fire, including St Paul's Cathedral. He is connected to our historical lore as one of the illustrious pupils taught by Dr. Richard Busby at Westminster School.

Reverend Edward Stone (1702–1768)

A Church of England cleric famous for discovering the active ingredient in aspirin. He is connected to our lineage because he served as the Justice of the Peace in Chipping Norton who examined our ancestor Benjamin Busby (Generation 7) and recorded his settlement certificate in 1765[cite: 17].

Sir Matt Busby (1909–1994)

The legendary Scottish football player and manager of Manchester United. While he shares the famous surname, he is a distant connection stemming from the Scottish branches of the name.
