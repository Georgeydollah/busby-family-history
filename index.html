The Busby Family History

A continuous legacy stretching from the 13th century to the present day. The earliest recorded Oxfordshire Busby was Alexander Busby of Milcombe, who purchased a 'hide' from Eynsham Abbey in 1241[cite: 21].

Direct Ancestral Line

Select a generation below to explore the lineage.

Generation 1: John Busby (Died c. 1558)

John Busby of the hamlet of Clifton was a husbandman and died circa 1558[cite: 21]. His will was proved at Oxford, and he requested his body to be buried in the churchyard of Deddington[cite: 21].

Generation 2: Simon Busby (Died c. 1598)

Simon Busby was a husbandman who died circa 1598[cite: 22]. A charitable man, he left ten shillings to the poor of Clifton and Deddington, and a bushel of barley to Deddington church[cite: 22].

Generation 3: William Busby (Died c. 1609)

William Busby was a husbandman buried in Deddington churchyard circa 1609[cite: 23]. His widow, Elizabeth, lived long enough to remarry, and her detailed will of 1656 was proved in the Court of Canterbury[cite: 24].

Generation 4: Simon Busby (Died 1650)

Simon Busby died in 1650[cite: 25]. He and his wife Anne had advanced to the yeoman class; the 1665 Oxfordshire Hearth Tax recorded Anne as having three hearths in her Clifton home, indicating a substantial property of six to eleven rooms[cite: 25].

Generation 5: Robert Busby (Baptized 1644)

Robert Busby was baptized in Deddington church in November 1644[cite: 28]. In 1682, he married Mary Reade of Enstone[cite: 28]. Mary is almost certainly the ancestor who purchased the family property in Hook Norton in 1702[cite: 29].

Generation 7: Benjamin Busby (Baptized 1721)

Baptized in Dunstew in 1721, this Benjamin proved to be the vital link connecting the Hook Norton family back to their Deddington roots[cite: 18]. This connection was discovered via a 1765 Settlement Certificate miraculously found under a bed in Hook Norton[cite: 18]!

Generation 8: Benjamin Busby (Baptized 1759)

Baptized in Hook Norton church in 1759, Benjamin continued the family's presence in the house/farm property now known as Laburnum Cottage on Queen Street[cite: 16]. He successfully diversified into the building trade[cite: 14].

Generation 9: Benjamin Busby (Baptized 1796)

Baptized in Hook Norton on May 5th 1796, Benjamin married Anna Prophett in 1822[cite: 12]. Anna was the daughter of a well-established Quaker family; her mother's side (the Harrises) were founder members of the 17th-century Quaker movement in Charlbury, Oxfordshire[cite: 12].

Generation 10: Henry Busby (Born 1825)

Henry Busby was born in 1825 in Garret Street, Hook Norton[cite: 9]. Following his father into the building trade, he became a master slater and plasterer, running his business from the family home[cite: 9]. He married Mary Ann Moulder in 1849[cite: 9].

Generation 11: Henry Busby (Born 1855)

Born in Hook Norton in 1855, Henry was a master slater and plasterer who founded the Hook Norton Temperance Band[cite: 4]. The band featured so many of his sons and grandsons that it was affectionately known locally as the 'Busby band'[cite: 4].

Dr. John Walter Busby

A doctor of medicine in Warwick who proudly solidified this branch of the family's legacy by successfully petitioning the College of Arms, resulting in an official Grant of Arms in 1976.

500 Years in Oxfordshire

For over half a millennium, the Busby family has left an indelible mark on the villages and towns of Oxfordshire. The family's journey reflects the evolution of English rural life, moving from husbandmen to yeoman farmers, and eventually establishing themselves as master builders and tradesmen.

  • Clifton & Deddington: The foundational roots of the family from the 1500s through the 17th century[cite: 21, 28].
  • Enstone & Great Rollright: The home of the Reade and Spelsbury families, who intermarried with the Busbys in the late 1600s and early 1700s[cite: 19, 28].
  • Dunstew & Middle Barton: Transitional homes for the family in the 18th century before firmly planting roots further west[cite: 18, 19].
  • Hook Norton: The true heartland of the modern family. From 1702 onwards, generations of Busbys operated as master slaters, plasterers, grocers, and musicians in this village[cite: 4, 9, 29].

Notable Figures & Connections

Sir John Busby

A prominent lawyer who worked for the Royalists and the Verney family in Buckinghamshire. In recognition of his service, he was granted a knighthood and a family crest. While he is not in our direct paternal lineage, his crest was proudly (and unofficially) adopted by many later Busbys who carved it onto their tombstones across Oxfordshire.

Dr. Richard Busby (1606–1695)

An English Anglican priest who served as the famously strict Headmaster of Westminster School for over 55 years. Among the many illustrious pupils he shaped was the legendary architect Sir Christopher Wren, the man responsible for rebuilding St Paul's Cathedral and 51 other churches after the Great Fire of London.

Reverend Edward Stone (1702–1768)

A Church of England cleric globally famous for discovering the active ingredient in aspirin. He is directly connected to our family history because he was the Justice of the Peace in Chipping Norton who examined our ancestor Benjamin Busby and signed his vital settlement certificate in 1765[cite: 17]!

The Harris Family of Charlbury

Through Anna Prophett (who married Benjamin Busby in 1822)[cite: 12], our family is tied to the Harrises. They are historically documented as founder members of the 17th-century Quaker movement in Charlbury, Oxfordshire[cite: 12], surviving a turbulent era of religious persecution.
