From the North: Our Viking Origins

The story of the Busby family begins long before our arrival in Oxfordshire. Etymologically and historically, the name "Busby" points to direct Nordic and Viking descent. Derived from Old Norse, "busk" refers to a shrub or bush, and the suffix "-by" denotes a farmstead or settlement.

During the era of the Danelaw, Viking settlers established roots in Yorkshire, founding the village of Great Busby. It was from these northern settlements that our ancestors eventually migrated south toward the Cotswolds, drawn by the booming agricultural economies of the abbeys.

The Lions of the Cotswolds & The Wool Trade

Upon migrating to Oxfordshire, the early Busbys found their fortune as sheep farmers and wool merchants working alongside the local monks and abbeys. The key to this wealth was a rare, majestic breed of sheep originally brought to the British Isles by the Romans: the Lions of the Cotswolds.

Renowned for their large size and extraordinary, silky fleeces, these sheep produced some of the highest quality wool in medieval Europe. The Busby ancestors capitalized on this, exporting the raw, silky wool directly to the weaving powerhouses of Florence, Italy. This international trade laid the financial foundation for the family's transition into the yeoman and master tradesmen classes.

Generational Consistencies & Traits

A deep analysis of over 500 years of family records reveals striking consistencies that define the Busby lineage:

  • Devotion to the Church: A continuous thread of church involvement runs through the centuries. From Simon Busby (1598) leaving barley to the Deddington church[cite: 22], to John Busby endowing church bells and altars[cite: 20], to Henry Busby (1855) founding the Hook Norton Temperance Band[cite: 4]. The family regularly sponsored pews, screens, and bell-ringing traditions.
  • The Building Trades: While they began as husbandmen and shepherds[cite: 21], by the 18th century, the family evolved into master tradesmen. The direct line is filled with master slaters, plasterers, carpenters, and glaziers[cite: 4, 6, 9].
  • Longevity: In eras plagued by high infant mortality, the Busby adults showed remarkable resilience. Many family members, such as William (died aged 78 in 1785) and Henry (died aged 77 in 1933), lived deep into their 70s and 80s[cite: 2, 15].
  • The "Busby Eyebrows": A famously strong, dark genetic trait passed down through the generations, so prominent that relatives often joked, "you can tell a Busby by their eyebrows!"

500 Years in Oxfordshire

Our ancestors left an indelible mark on Oxfordshire's landscape:

  • Clifton & Deddington: The foundational roots from the 1500s through the 17th century[cite: 21, 28].
  • Enstone & Great Rollright: Home of the Reade and Spelsbury families, who intermarried with the Busbys[cite: 19, 28].
  • Dunstew & Middle Barton: Transitional homes for the family in the 18th century before firmly planting roots further west[cite: 18, 19].
  • Hook Norton: The true heartland of the modern family. From 1702 onwards, generations operated as master slaters and musicians here[cite: 4, 9, 29].

Notable Figures & Connections

Dr. Richard Busby (1606–1695)

An Anglican priest who served as the famously strict Headmaster of Westminster School for over 55 years. Among the many illustrious pupils he shaped was the legendary architect Sir Christopher Wren, the man responsible for rebuilding St Paul's Cathedral and 51 other churches after the Great Fire of London.

Sir John Busby

A prominent lawyer who worked for the Royalists and the Verney family. He was granted a knighthood and a family crest. While he is not in our direct paternal lineage, his crest was proudly (if unofficially) adopted by many later Busby tradesmen who carved it onto their tombstones across Oxfordshire.

Dr. John Walter Busby & The Official Crest

While the earlier generations "borrowed" the arms of Sir John Busby, Dr. John Walter Busby (a doctor of medicine in Warwick) proudly solidified this specific branch of the family's legacy. He successfully petitioned the College of Arms and was granted a true, official Coat of Arms in 1976.

Original Archival Transcripts

Below are the raw, verbatim transcripts from the primary historical research.

Generation 1
JOHN BUSBY of CLIFTON, DEDDINGTON 1558[cite: 21]
John Busby of the hamlet of Clifton, Deddington, Oxfordshire is the first proven direct ancestor... He was a husbandman and died circa 1558. His Will was proved at Oxford in that year. He requested his body to be buried in Deddington churchyard.[cite: 21]

Generation 2
SIMON BUSBY of CLIFTON, DEDDINGTON 1598[cite: 20]
Simon Busby, our direct ancestor, was a husbandman like his father, John. He died circa 1598 and his Will proved at Oxford. Simon left 10 shillings for the poor of Clifton and Deddington and a bushell of barley to Deddington church.[cite: 22]

Generation 3
WILLIAM BUSBY of CLIFTON, DEDDINGTON 1609[cite: 23]
William Busby our direct ancestor, was also a husbandman and died circa 1609. He was buried in Deddington churchyard. We also know that his wife was called Elizabeth and she lived long enough to re-marry.[cite: 23]

Generation 4
SIMON BUSBY of DEDDINGTON 1650[cite: 25]
Simon Busby of Deddington our direct ancestor, died in 1650. Entry of his burial at Deddington is in the parish register. Anne is also mentioned in the Oxfordshire Hearth Tax 1665 as having 3 hearths in Clifton, Deddington. This meant that she probably lived in a house with six to eleven rooms and was of the yeoman class...[cite: 25]

Generation 5
ROBERT BUSBY of DEDDINGTON baptised 1644[cite: 28]
Robert Busby our direct ancestor was baptised in Deddington church November 24th 1644. Our Robert married Mary Reade of Enstone on 4th January 1682 at Heythrop.[cite: 28] However our Mary Reade who became the wife of Robert Busby, is almost certainly the lady who bought the family property in Hook Norton in 1702...[cite: 29]

Generation 6
ROBERT BUSBY baptised DEDDINGTON April 1684[cite: 19]
Robert Busby our direct ancestor, the eldest son of Robert and Mary, was baptised in Deddington church. He married Sarah Spelsbury of Enstone at Kiddington in 1705.[cite: 19]

Generation 7
BENJAMIN BUSBY baptised Dunstew 1721[cite: 18]
Benjamin Busby our direct ancestor proved to be the link that enabled me to trace our ancestry back from Hook Norton to Deddington... provided the vital link. It was a Settlement Certificate for Benjamin Busby.[cite: 18] The J.P. who examined Benjamin, Edward Stone, was actually also the clergyman of Chipping Norton who had first described the analgesic powers of willow bark i.e. he was the person who discovered aspirin...[cite: 17]

Generation 8
BENJAMIN BUSBY of HOOK NORTON baptized 1759[cite: 15]
Benjamin, our direct ancestor, the third son of Benjamin and Ann Nixon, was baptized in Hook Norton church in 1759. Although he was not the eldest son of Benjamin and Ann, he must have been the one who continued in the house/farm property that is now, Laburnum Cottage, Queen Street, HN.[cite: 15] We do have one small piece of documentation about this Benjamin which indicates that by this generation the Busbys had diversified into the building trade...[cite: 14]

Generation 9
BENJAMIN BUSBY of HOOK NORTON baptized 1796[cite: 12]
Benjamin our direct ancestor was baptized in Hook Norton church on May 5th 1796. He married Anna Prophett of Sibford Ferris... The family tree of this branch is extensive and I do have a copy but the Harrises and Busbys are mentioned in the Victoria County History of Oxford as being founder members of the Quaker movement in the 17th century in Charlbury, Oxfordshire.[cite: 12]

Generation 10
HENRY BUSBY of HOOK NORTON b1825[cite: 10]
Henry our direct ancestor was born May 13th 1825 in the family home in what is now called Queen Street but was originally Garret Street, Hook Norton. Henry followed his father Benjamin into the building trade. On 15th April 1849 he married Mary Ann Moulder...[cite: 9]

Generation 11
HENRY (Born 1855)[cite: 5]
Henry, our direct ancestor, was born in Hook Norton in 1855. He followed his father into the building trade becoming a master slater and plasterer.[cite: 4] Henry was musical and was said to have founded the Hook Norton Temperance Band (later the Hook Norton Band). In fact all his sons were instrumentalists in the band from quite an early age, followed by his grandsons. It was even called the 'Busby band'.[cite: 4]
